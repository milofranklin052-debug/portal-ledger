# portal-ledger
Project Brief: In this project I am setting out to answer a question that grows more important by the day: What do college football programs actually get for their money in the transfer portal and NIL era? I will also answer four important sub-questions to help me come up with a more concise final response. Firstly, how do portal signees and high school commits compare in performance for cost? Secondly, does more spending lead to more wins, and when do those returns flatten out? Thirdly, how has revenue sharing actually altered roster strategy and ideologies? Finally, how should a mid-sized FBS program allocate their limited resources in roster construction? To answer this question I will begin by looking at year one of this NIL-crazed era, the 2021-22 season. I will cover every transfer portal season following all the way to the modern day season, 2026-27. I will also be covering every position in football from quarterback to kicker. It will be important to look at both in-season performance and how it affects the offseason of programs of different sizes. The idea is to look for trends or patterns in the portal, not necessarily particular players or situations. I would rather know generally that cornerback is a very good position to invest in for signees than have knowledge about particular seasons of cornerback production.. My final product will be a public website that displays my findings in a concise way, but also offers the opportunity for real fans to dive into the intricacies of my project. I will also create a research report that describes the processes I took to come to my final conclusion, and a memo that I can send to athletic directors to help them in their construction of their 2028-29 rosters.
